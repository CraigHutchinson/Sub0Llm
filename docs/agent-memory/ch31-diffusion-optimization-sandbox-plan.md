User idea (2026-06-15): a dedicated chapter / isolated sandbox to understand what the OPTIMIZER is really doing for text diffusion, since "we are not learning a single token like autoregressive" — each diffusion step predicts MANY masked tokens at once at variable noise, which may make each step less effective. Ch09 covers optimizer mechanics (SGD/Adam/clip); nothing covers diffusion-SPECIFIC optimization dynamics. Goal: determine if we need a better optimizer, a reweighted loss, a different noise schedule, or a more fundamental model/objective change.

**Empirical motivation (observed live):** EVERY optimizer (Adam/AdamW/Muon) sits in the UNIGRAM BASIN (NELBO ≈5.9 = the token-frequency prior) for THOUSANDS of steps before escaping — Adam ~5.9 at step 1000, fixed-Muon still ~5.88 at step ~2500. This sticky basin is the pathology to study. [[ch29-model-quality-founded-dims]] shows the model is optimization-limited (mild undertraining had slow continued gains).

**Why diffusion ≠ AR optimization (hypothesis to test):** AR predicts 1 next token/position with consistent left-context → low-variance, directionally-stable gradient. Diffusion masks a RANDOM fraction (variable t), predicts ALL masked positions at once from BIDIRECTIONAL context → per-step gradient is an average over a random masking config: HIGH variance, mixes easy (low-t, much context) with hard (high-t, little context) whose gradients may partially CANCEL. The 1/t weight fixes the expectation, not the variance.

**FIRST SANDBOX RESULT (built `--grad-probe` in ch29 main; cosine of mean gradient per noise level + within-level consistency):** the hypothesis is CONFIRMED as VARIANCE + DIVERSITY, not destructive conflict. At RANDOM INIT (the basin): within-level consistency at t=0.5 = **0.31** (a single masking's grad has only 0.31 cosine to the mean → high variance), and easy↔hard gradients (t=0.10↔0.75) are nearly ORTHOGONAL (cosine **0.09**, adjacent levels 0.46–0.79). At CONVERGED (completeb): within-level consistency crashes to **0.026** and all cross-level cosines ≈0 — the signature of being AT A MINIMUM (mean grad→0, per-sample = pure sampling noise), which INDEPENDENTLY cross-confirms the "essentially converged" undertraining finding. NO negative cosines → the curriculum's "hard fights easy" premise is too strong; the real issue is a noisy, diluted average. **Highest-leverage lever indicated: VARIANCE REDUCTION** (more maskings averaged per step, especially at correlated/adjacent t; antithetic masks; multiple t per window) — note the current data-parallel step averages 4 windows but EACH AT A DIFFERENT t, diluting across the noise-level diversity. The curriculum helps by narrowing t (raising within-step consistency) but is biased. `--grad-probe` is fast (~10s, standalone) and reusable for any checkpoint/init.

**VARIANCE-REDUCTION INTERVENTIONS (user idea: even/deterministic distribution, not randomness — the critical factor is unbiased even coverage):** two independent variance sources, each with a deterministic fix:
1. **EXACT-COUNT masking (`--exact-noise`, BUILT + tested + MEASURED):** mask EXACTLY round(t·T) positions (partial Fisher-Yates) instead of Bernoulli-per-position. Removes the COUNT variance (realised noise = nominal exactly → exact model conditioning), drops the min-1 floor hack. **grad-probe MEASURED a BIG win: within-level consistency 0.311 → 0.508 at random init (63% less directional noise) — without needing a training run.** Strongly validates the user's "even distribution" thesis. (Note: completeB was single-thread = 1 window/step = highest variance; threaded W=4 already reduces it 4×.) Training A/B (does the cleaner gradient escape the basin faster / reach better recall?) running: 2×2 of {Bernoulli, exact} × {diff-t, shared-t}.
2. **STRATIFIED / EVEN t (next):** `--shared-t` (one t/step, all W workers share it → sharpens the per-t gradient) is a crude first version, mid-A/B and NOT a clear win so far. Better = STRATIFIED t (W workers' t evenly spread across the range each step → unbiased lower-variance estimate of the objective's t-integral), or a per-epoch shuffled even-t schedule. Addresses the near-orthogonal cross-level diversity (easy↔hard cosine 0.09).

**SINGLE-THREAD BATCHED PATH as a VARIANCE-REDUCTION vehicle (user idea, backlog):** compute B windows in one batched GEMM (`batched_diffusion_loss` already does B·T-row stacking) on ONE core + average — decouples "effective batch for a clean gradient" from core count. As a pure throughput play it's only ~+15% ([[ch29-training-throughput-findings]]: bandwidth-bound, weights L3-resident so batching just amortizes overhead) — NOT the "massive single-core boost" hoped. BUT it's the right END-STATE for the validated variance reductions: batched B windows + `--shared-t` + `--exact-noise` in one efficient step. Re-measure the batched throughput on the AVX-512 build (the +15% may be stale). Single-thread is also the HIGHEST-variance regime (completeB was single-thread = 1 window/step), so batch-averaging there has the most to gain.

**Sandbox experiments (concrete, measurable):**
1. Characterize the basin: NELBO/recall vs step for SGD/Adam/AdamW/Muon on a tiny controlled task; escape-time; show basin = frequency-prior plateau.
2. Measure the gradient pathology: variance across random masks at FIXED weights; cosine similarity of low-t vs high-t gradients (do they conflict?); step-to-step direction consistency vs an AR baseline.
3. Interventions: easy-first noise schedule ([[ch28-curriculum-findings]] NoiseCurriculum); loss reweighting (per-position / token-frequency / confidence — [[ch29-training-methodology-backlog]]); VARIANCE REDUCTION (multiple t or antithetic masks per step); stronger t-conditioning; optimizer choice.
4. Analytically-tractable toy: a tiny vocab where the optimal denoiser is computable → visualize the loss landscape + how close each optimizer gets.

**Reuses already-built infra:** `--optimizer adam|adamw|muon` + `make_optimizer` + tested `newton_schulz_orthogonalize` ([[optimizer-alternatives-backlog]]); the proxy A/B rig (disjoint-core via `--pin`); `--inspect`/`--track-recall`. Result so far: AdamW BEATS Adam (+1.4pt word-level) — decoupled weight decay improves conditioning, not just regularization; Muon naive all-2D collapsed (embedding-orthogonalization), fixed routing (embedding→AdamW) under test. Pairs with the additive-track plan [[ch28-diffusion-track-plan]].

**UNIFIED (B,W) TRAINING PATH (2026-06-15, commit 307b42d).** Legacy cleanup per user: the serial / data-parallel-pool / single-thread-batched paths were THREE divergent code paths that WELDED effective batch B (gradient consistency ∝√B, the QUALITY knob) to worker count W (parallelism/throughput). Now ONE path (`ParallelTrainer` in `diffusion/include/sub0diff/train/parallel.hpp`): W workers cooperatively process B windows/step (B/W each via `batched_diffusion_loss`); master gradient = mean over all B, IDENTICAL for any W. Single-thread = W=1 (a pool of one). `--batch B` is now INDEPENDENT of `--threads W` (default B=W = historical pool; B rounds up to a multiple of W). So √16 (B=16) runs on 1, 4, or 16 cores — same gradient, only wall-time changes. Per-window DETERMINISTIC seeding (splitmix64 over step+GLOBAL-window-index) makes a window's corruption a pure function of its global index, not which worker ran it → VERIFIED: W∈{1,2,4,8} at B=8 give IDENTICAL held-out NELBO (5.2318) while wall-time drops 2.0s→0.4s. New invariance test + whole_word parity in batched. Note `--batch` single-thread (W=1) is still ~6× slower/window than spreading B over W cores — so to raise consistency, raise B and pick W for the throughput you want.

**CONSISTENCY LEVER CONFIRMED at scale (2026-06-15).** The sandbox said gradient within-level consistency (∝√W of the data-parallel pool's shared-t average) is THE lever. Tested on the real model, full corpus, OLD default arch (D=128/L8), self-terminating, only `--threads` differing: √4 (default, 4 workers) → **19.0% overall / 14.6% word-START / 42.2%@10%**; √16 (16 workers) → **21.5% / 16.9% / 48.4%@10%** (+2.5pt / +2.3pt / +6.2pt; best held-out NELBO 3.712→3.519). Improvement is consistent across EVERY noise level and breakdown (real signal, not variance). √16 also trained productively 2.3× longer (119840 vs ~52k steps) — cleaner gradient keeps improving past where √4 stalls. EFFICIENT vehicle = more pool workers at full throughput (NOT the `--batch` single-thread path, which profiled ~6× slower per window: the cost is the batched transformer backward over B·T rows, not the CE reduction). Caveat: single-run each (rigorous = seed-average); √16 cost ~2.7h wall. Open: does the default `threads=4` (throughput-motivated) want raising for quality? quality/throughput tradeoff to surface.

**BUILT (2026-06-15) — `diffusion/chapters/ch31_optimizer_sandbox/`, runs in seconds.** Isolated controls driving the PRODUCTION Adam/AdamW/Muon on a synthetic of the exact pathology: matrix W, target W*, T noise levels = disjoint COLUMN BLOCKS (per-level grads exactly orthogonal, sum to truth), within-level noise σ as the consistency knob {1.0..0.3}, shared-t vs independent-t, plus a depth-2 `Y=W₂·W₁` control and an LR-sensitivity sweep. **RESULT — the "Muon fails because orthogonalization amplifies the high-variance gradient" explanation is FALSIFIED:** (1) Muon does NOT fail on the noise/cross-orthogonality — tracks Adam within ~15% at every consistency level (muon/adam 0.73–1.16), no blow-up; (2) depth-2 coupling doesn't break it either (0.72–1.16×); (3) Adam and Muon are SIMILARLY LR-sensitive (best→worst Adam 0.12→0.94, Muon 0.11→0.84) and Muon's ch29 lr 0.02 is its BEST on the toy; (4) the consistency-collapse near convergence (0.50→0.07, mirrors the real 0.31→0.026) is REPRODUCED and is OPTIMIZER-INDEPENDENT — final residual rises as c0 falls (~0.05→~0.19) equally for both. **Two load-bearing conclusions:** (a) the optimizer is NOT the crux — Adam≈Muon everywhere, swapping doesn't move the wall; (b) gradient WITHIN-LEVEL CONSISTENCY is the crux and bounds both optimizers identically → the lever for the next recall gain is RAISING CONSISTENCY further (more exact/even corruption, per-t variance reduction, larger effective batch per level — the batched single-thread path), NOT a new optimizer. ch29's catastrophic real Muon (NELBO 5.68, word-START 0.2%) is therefore a full-transformer effect the toy omits (6-layer coupling / RMSNorm-softmax-residual signal propagation / embedding-output AdamW-group tuning / no warmup); deserves ONE fair real re-run (warmup + small matrix-LR sweep) before closing. ch29 already routes embedding/output→AdamW at adamw_grp_lr=1e-3 (not the starved 3e-4).
